# KRIBU Brain — Five Demo Stories with WOW Moments

The WOW factor should come from KRIBU discovering something the user didn't explicitly know to look for, connecting clues across documents, and then doing something useful about it.

This version upgrades the five demo stories around evidence-backed discovery, useful outputs, and human-approved next actions.

---

## 1. NGO Project: Where Did the Progress Go?

### The Hook
A national development project has been running for three years. The latest six-monthly report shows that expenditure is progressing, but field-level achievements are falling behind targets. Management needs to know why.

### The KRIBU Demo

1. The project director asks:  
   > “Review the last three half-yearly progress reports and expenditure statements. Tell me where we're falling behind and why.”

2. KRIBU compares targets, reported achievements, expenditure, and unresolved issues across the documents.

3. KRIBU discovers that two activities have repeatedly missed their targets across consecutive reporting periods, while expenditure has continued. It connects those delays to documented issues in field reports, such as delayed equipment delivery or staffing gaps, **if supported by the sample data**.

4. The director asks:  
   > “Prepare a management briefing showing the biggest concerns, supporting evidence, and questions we need to resolve.”

5. KRIBU generates a briefing and prepares follow-up tasks for approval.

### WOW Moment
KRIBU doesn't just summarize six months of reports. It spots a pattern across three years of project history and explains what the documents actually support.

## 2. Client Servicing Excellence: Seeing the Pattern Behind Every Complaint

### The Hook
An organization receives customer complaints through multiple interactions and channels. Each case may appear isolated, but recurring issues can reveal unmet expectations, service gaps, and opportunities to improve the customer experience.

### The KRIBU Demo

1. The user asks:  
   > “Analyze these customer complaints and previous case records. Identify recurring issues and tell me where our service experience is falling short.”

2. KRIBU retrieves relevant case histories, categorizes complaints, and groups similar issues across customers.

3. KRIBU identifies recurring pain points, highlights repeated unresolved concerns, and connects them to documented customer interactions.

4. The user asks:  
   > “Summarize the key service improvement opportunities and recommend actions to prevent these complaints from recurring.”

5. KRIBU generates an evidence-backed service insights report and prepares follow-up tasks for the relevant teams.

### WOW Moment
KRIBU turns complaint history into actionable customer experience intelligence, helping organizations move beyond resolving individual cases to understanding recurring customer frustrations and improving service proactively.

## 3. Procurement: The Cheapest Supplier Isn't the Best Deal

### The Hook
A procurement officer receives three quotations. Supplier A has the lowest headline price, and management expects a quick comparison before approving a purchase.

### The KRIBU Demo

1. The officer asks:  
   > “Compare these quotations and identify the best option against our requirements, not just the lowest price.”

2. KRIBU extracts prices, delivery timelines, warranty terms, and exclusions.

3. KRIBU discovers that the lowest-priced quotation excludes an important requirement or has a shorter warranty than the others. It highlights the price difference alongside the omitted term.

4. The officer asks:  
   > “Prepare a comparison for management and draft clarification questions for the supplier.”

5. KRIBU generates an evidence-backed comparison and the clarification email.

### WOW Moment
KRIBU surfaces a commercially important detail buried in the quotation instead of simply ranking suppliers by price.

## 4. Sales: The Top Clients You Forgot to Contact

### The Hook
Eid is approaching. The sales director wants to maintain relationships with the company's most valuable clients and distributors, but the team has a long customer list and limited time.

### The KRIBU Demo

1. The director asks:  
   > “Find our top 10 clients by revenue over the last 12 months that we haven't contacted recently.”

2. KRIBU analyzes a sample CRM export containing client revenue, contact history, and relevant dates.

3. KRIBU identifies high-value clients with no recent recorded outreach and prioritizes the list according to the director's criteria.

4. The director asks:  
   > “Draft personalized Eid greetings for these clients based on their relationship with us, then send them to me for approval.”

5. KRIBU prepares the emails, presents them for review, and sends only after the required approval.

### WOW Moment
KRIBU doesn't merely generate ten greetings. It identifies whom the team should contact, explains why they were selected, personalizes the messages, and prepares the next action.

## 5. HR: The Complete Employee History in One Question

### The Hook
HR is reviewing employee records for an internal audit or compensation review. An employee has worked at the company for several years, but their appointment letter, salary revisions, and benefit documents are scattered across folders.

### The KRIBU Demo

1. The HR manager asks:  
   > “Prepare this employee's complete service history, including tenure, salary revisions, and applicable benefits.”

2. KRIBU retrieves the relevant employee records and current HR policies.

3. KRIBU builds a timeline of joining date and documented revisions, calculates the change between recorded salaries, and identifies missing revision letters or inconsistencies between documents.

4. The manager asks:  
   > “Prepare an HR review summary and list anything that needs verification before we finalize the record.”

5. KRIBU generates the summary with references to the source documents and a checklist of unresolved items.

### WOW Moment
KRIBU reconstructs a fragmented employee history and catches missing or inconsistent records that would otherwise require manual investigation.
